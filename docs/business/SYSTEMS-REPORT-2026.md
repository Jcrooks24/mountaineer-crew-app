SYSTEMS REPORT

Mountaineer Moving Co.

Tools, Workflows, and Integration Gaps

## Purpose

This document records the tools used across the business: what is working, what is not, and which built-in features remain unused. It is intended to inform systems integration with M1 Logistics in the event of a merger.

## Introduction

Most systems audits start by listing the tools a business uses and asking whether each one is any good. This document takes the opposite approach. It starts from the work itself: the set of things Mountaineer Moving has to accomplish to run, regardless of what software happens to be doing them today. Only then does it ask which tools serve each of those jobs, how well, and where the gaps are.

The reason for the reframe is practical. When you organize around tools, redundancy and missing capabilities are easy to miss, because each tool looks reasonable on its own. When you organize around the work, the picture sharpens immediately: you can see where three different systems are all doing one job, where a tool is stretched to cover something it was never built for, and where a job is getting done by memory and ad-hoc effort because no tool owns it at all.

The work breaks down into the following core functions:

- Generate demand and capture leads
- Manage the sales pipeline and convert leads
- Communicate with customers and crew
- Schedule and dispatch jobs
- Execute the job and capture field data
- Bill, collect, and track payments
- Handle damage claims
- Run payroll and manage HR
- Maintain the fleet
- Stay FMCSA and DOT compliant
- Manage materials inventory
- Keep the books
- Measure performance and report
- Manage vendors and partners
The sections that follow work through these functions and the tools currently assigned to each. The goal is to inform systems integration with M1 Logistics in the event of a merger by showing not just what we use, but what we are actually trying to do and how well our current setup does it.

## FMCSA Compliance

### Crew App: RODS, DVIRs, Prior On-Duty Statements, TRALA

Why We Use It

The Mountaineer Crew App handles four federal trucking compliance requirements entirely on crew phones rather than on paper, gating or prompting each form at the right moment so nothing gets missed.

DVIRs (Driver Vehicle Inspection Reports) are the pre- and post-trip checklists every driver completes in the DVIR tab to confirm a truck’s brakes, lights, tires, and other systems are safe before and after each trip. This is required under 49 CFR 396.11, and any noted defect triggers a mechanic review queue.

RODS (Record of Duty Status) is the federally required daily log of a driver’s duty status (off duty, sleeper berth, driving, on duty not driving). Crew fill it out under Profile, then Long Distance, on interstate trips to document hours-of-service compliance.

The Prior On-Duty Hours Statement is a companion form under that same Long Distance menu, filed before departure on interstate jobs, where the driver declares hours worked over the prior 7 days and 24 hours per FMCSA 395.8(j)(2).

TRALA is the moving industry’s exemption (the movers exemption) that reduces the RODS burden for qualifying interstate moving jobs. The app documents it so a driver can produce it during a roadside inspection.

Summary

| Overall Pros | Overall Cons |
|---|---|
| Mobile friendly and electronic. Streamlined to be as easy as possible. | DOT requirements have been difficult to implement, because existing drivers are not used to the new systems and steps. |

Because the knowledge is vendor-held, switching providers or bringing the work in-house later is harder. Valuable, but the most expensive line item to leave unmanaged. |

### Most Painful Gaps

These are the missing systems that cost us money or create friction now, ranked by how directly they hit the bottom line. The first three all touch getting paid.

| Gap | Why It Hurts |

The deduction relies on memory, is frequently forgotten, and creates friction at billing, which means real dollars are at risk every time it slips. |

### Directional Changes

The clearest direction for the business is to keep consolidating tools and capabilities, building on the infrastructure already in place rather than adding more standalone systems. Several features are under consideration for the crew app to help with consolidation.

The first is a set of additional scheduling tools to extend what the app already does for crew availability and assignment.

The most significant opportunity is heavier integration with both SmartMoving and QuickBooks. Wiring the app directly into SmartMoving on one side and QuickBooks on the other would let it address many of the pain points described above, pulling job and lead data from the CRM and pushing clean billing and expense data into the accounting system without the manual re-entry and reconciliation that currently spreads billing across three systems.

A further build under consideration is trip routing: logging driving time automatically and routing crews to avoid low-clearance obstructions and high-traffic areas, which would also feed cleaner mileage and drive-leg data back into the reporting layer.

On the claims side, the move is to strengthen the moving contract first. More robust, proactive claims language sets us up to implement a formal claims process down the line, giving us a documented basis to attach claims to jobs and handle them consistently rather than case by case.

Finally, there is interest in evaluating a different CRM altogether. SmartMoving's limited customization and integration library mean we are not able to use the full feature set we want, and a platform better suited to how we actually work may be worth the switching cost over the long term.

## Appendix: Mountaineer Crew App

Comprehensive Description: Features and Behavior

#### What the App Is

The Mountaineer Crew App is the digital backbone of every job day for Mountaineer Moving Co. It replaces paper logs, manual timesheets, and verbal materials tracking with a single shared tool that crew and admin use in real time. It is a Progressive Web App (PWA): it runs in the phone’s browser and is added to the home screen like a native app, with no app store download. All data syncs to a shared Google Sheet that admin uses for billing, compliance, and job review. Crew never see this sheet directly; they just log events, and the app handles the rest.

The app no longer uses numbered versions. Each build has a friendly two-word name (for example, “Brave Otter”) shown on the Profile tab alongside a smaller build ID. Both are used when reporting a problem, so support can pinpoint the exact build.

#### Roles

There are two roles with different levels of access.

Crew (all drivers and movers) can log timeline events, complete DVIRs, track materials, take photos, fill out the job report and bill, complete long-distance compliance forms, browse the document library, and submit expense and reimbursement requests.

Admin (Jacob and designated employees) can do everything crew can do, plus activate accounts, sign off on mechanic DVIR reviews (in person or by emailed link), manage notes and documents, view the live crew map, review job summaries, log office hours, use advanced settings, and build estimates. Admins cannot modify their own employee record.

#### Offline Behavior

Almost everything works without cell signal. The app queues inputs locally and syncs automatically when signal returns; it never tells a user to “go online and try again” mid-job.

Works offline (queued and synced on reconnect): timeline events, timeline edits (handled by a separate patch queue), photos, materials add and remove, DVIR submission, long-distance forms, the job report and bill, expense and reimbursement requests, admin office hours, and, partially, the admin notes display, which shows cached data from the last fetch.

Requires signal (online only): login, signup, and password reset; the calendar job picker; and document library downloads (though the cached list can still be browsed offline).

Partial: the estimator (item adds queue offline, but customer details and metadata autosave need a connection).

A critical rule follows from this: pick your job from the calendar before losing signal, because the calendar picker is online only. If you are heading somewhere with no service, select the job while still at dispatch.

#### Getting Started: Installation and Accounts

Installation takes about five minutes and is done once. The result is a home screen icon that opens the app like any native app.

On iPhone, Safari is mandatory. Chrome and other iOS browsers do not support “Add to Home Screen.” Open the app URL in Safari, tap Share, scroll down, tap “Add to Home Screen,” and confirm. On Android, use Chrome: open the URL, tap the three-dot menu, tap “Add to Home Screen” or “Install App,” and confirm. After install, the app keeps users signed in between job days and updates itself in the background, with a manual force-update option on the Profile tab.

New crew self-register at the app URL by entering name, email, and password and tapping Create Account. The account stays inactive until an admin activates it, so a new member must text Jacob immediately; they cannot log in until activation. Once activated, the account is saved permanently.

Sessions stay active until the user explicitly logs out, so there are no repeated logins on the same device between job days. Logout is on the Profile tab, which also offers “Sign out all devices” to immediately invalidate the session everywhere. Forgotten passwords are reset via a link emailed from the login screen. Reset links expire after 15 minutes, and an “invalid or expired” message means the link timed out or came from a different environment, so request a fresh link rather than reusing an old one.

#### Profile Tab

The Profile tab is a landing screen for the account, app tools, and update status, not just a settings form. The user’s photo appears as the “created by” avatar on Timeline events, so admin can see who logged what.

My Profile: the user’s name, photo, and email sit in a card at the top; tapping it opens a dedicated screen for updating name and photo.

Update behavior: a prominent “Update app to latest version” button forces the app to pull the newest build, reporting “You’re on the latest version,” “Update found, reloading,” or “You’re offline, can’t check for updates.” This is rarely needed, because the app checks for updates on its own, periodically while open and whenever the user switches back to it. When a new version finishes installing in the background, a bottom banner reads “New version available”; tapping Update at a safe stopping point applies it, and ignoring it is safe because the update applies on its own the next time the app is fully closed and reopened.

Tools and Resources card: groups every feature that does not live on the Timeline tab, including Scheduling Availability (with an overdue warning badge), Log Expense / Reimbursement, Long Distance Compliance forms, and the Document Library.

Patch Notes at the bottom list recent changes, each shown as a short preview with a “Read more” link, so the tab stays scannable as the list grows.

#### FMCSA Compliance

Mountaineer operates commercial vehicles and must comply with FMCSA (Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration) rules on every trip. These are federal requirements: DVIRs are required by 49 CFR 396.11 on every pre- and post-trip, and long-distance forms are required by 49 CFR 395.8 on every interstate trip. Non-compliance can fall on both the company and the individual driver. The app handles all required forms digitally.

#### DVIR (Driver Vehicle Inspection Report)

A DVIR is a written record that a driver inspected their vehicle and noted any defects. It is required on every trip, by every driver, with no exceptions. The checklist covers 25 items (brakes, lights, tires, mirrors, horn, wipers, fuel and oil levels, coupling devices, emergency equipment, and more), each marked satisfactory or defective.

For a pre-trip, the driver opens the DVIR tab before moving the truck, selects the vehicle from the admin-configured unit dropdown, reviews the previous DVIR for prior defects and their resolution, walks the 25-item checklist, describes any defects in the notes field, signs in the signature pad, and submits. A clean pre-trip takes about two minutes. The post-trip is the same process, done after the last job of the day when the truck is returned.

Defect handling: if a DVIR notes any defect, it enters the mechanic review queue and admin is notified. The truck should not be driven until a mechanic signs the mechanic-review portion; once signed, the DVIR clears and the truck is good to go. If unsure whether a defect has been cleared, check with Jacob before taking the truck out. Mechanic sign-off happens two ways, both admin-initiated: in person (an admin opens the DVIR in DVIR Review and signs directly on the device) or remotely (an admin emails a secure sign-off link, scoped to that one DVIR, to a mechanic without an app account, who reviews and signs from their own device).

Multi-job days on the same truck: one morning pre-trip covers all jobs, and one post-trip at the end of the last job covers all jobs. When the DVIR reminder pops up between jobs, the driver selects the appropriate multi-job option so the app stops prompting: “Already pre-tripped for an earlier job today” on the second-plus job, or “Multiple jobs today, final DVIR at end of last job” when the post-trip prompt fires early.

#### Long Distance Forms (interstate jobs only)

When a job crosses state lines, additional hours-of-service rules apply, and two forms live under Profile, then Long Distance.

The Prior On-Duty Hours Statement (FMCSA 395.8(j)(2)) is required before departure on any interstate trip. The driver declares hours worked for each of the past 7 days and the prior 24 hours, then signs and submits before the truck leaves.

The Record of Duty Status (RODS) is a daily log of duty status with timestamps for every change. The four statuses are Off Duty, Sleeper Berth, Driving, and On Duty (Not Driving). The driver logs each change as it happens, adds remarks for notable events (fuel stops, inspections, loading and unloading), and signs and submits at end of day.

The app also documents the TRALA movers exemption, under which some interstate moving jobs qualify for a reduced RODS requirement. During a traffic stop, an enforcement officer may ask the driver to produce the TRALA document embedded in the app.

#### Job Day Workflow

The workflow runs in order on every job.

Before leaving the yard, the crew first pick the job from the calendar dropdown at the top of the Timeline tab while still online. This matters because the picker pulls the job from Google Calendar and assigns a unique ID shared across all crew on the same job. If two crew pick the same calendar event, their logs automatically merge into one record, whereas manual entries with different names create two separate records that admin must reconcile. With no signal, the crew use “Other / Manual entry” and coordinate so everyone types the name identically. Next, the driver completes the pre-trip DVIR before the truck moves, and on interstate jobs completes the Prior On-Duty Hours Statement before departure.

On the road and on the job, the crew tap START when pulling away from dispatch (this is when billable time begins, not before), then tap Arrive at every location and Depart every time they leave. This applies to every stop: origin, destination, storage facilities, dump sites, and any intermediate stop, because admin use the data to reconcile hours and mileage. A common mistake is logging only the main job site and forgetting intermediate stops. Throughout, materials are logged as the job progresses (tap a catalog item or use custom entry, set quantity, remove by swiping or tapping delete), and photos are taken to document the job, captioned where relevant for fragile items, pre-existing damage, or company-caused damage. Photos are automatically compressed before upload; a photo that appears stuck is simply offline and will sync on reconnect, so leave it alone rather than retrying.

At end of job, the crew tap FINISH on returning to dispatch (this ends billable time), and the driver completes the post-trip DVIR after the final job of the day. The crew lead then completes the Job Report and reviews and submits the Bill.

#### Timeline Tab

The Timeline is the core crew tool, logging every job event in chronological order. Each event captures a timestamp (Mountain time), GPS location, the user’s identity, and an optional note, logged by tapping the appropriate action button (Start, Arrive, Depart, Break Start, Break End, Finish, and so on).

Event times can be corrected after the fact for honest mistakes, such as a forgotten tap or a wrong phone clock, by tapping the time on an event, adjusting, and saving. A time can be set up to 7 days back from when the event was originally captured and up to 5 minutes ahead of now. The original capture time is always preserved on the admin side; editing does not erase it, and admin can see both.

Every event has a note button for adding or editing a note on that specific event, and notes sync across devices, so a note added on one phone appears on all others on the same job after a refresh. Separately, a job-wide “Notes” card below the activity list holds one freeform note for the whole job (for example, “customer added a stop we didn’t know about”). It autosaves a couple of seconds after typing stops, syncs across devices, and shows a status indicator (Saving, Saved, or “Saved offline, will sync”).

Every time displayed anywhere in the app is Mountain time (Bozeman, MT) regardless of the phone’s timezone, so what crew see matches exactly what admin sees. Timeline events log fully offline and queue for sync; the local log retains roughly 14 days of history capped at 2,000 entries, with the Google Sheet as the authoritative long-term record.

#### Materials

Materials used on a job feed directly into the Bill as line items. To add, the user opens the Materials tab, searches the catalog or uses custom entry, sets quantity, and taps Add; the item appears immediately via optimistic UI. To remove, the user swipes or taps the remove icon, and removals sync to the admin sheet with no ghost rows; a pending add that has not synced yet can be removed before it ever reaches the server. Catalog items carry an admin-set unit price (total = quantity times unit price), while custom items allow a manually typed cost. All materials appear as line items in the bill.

#### Bill Calculator

The bill is embedded in the Job Report tab and auto-seeds from job data, so the crew lead can finalize charges before submission. It auto-populates labor hours (calculated from Start/Finish pairs per crew member, one labor row per person on multi-crew jobs), materials (every logged item as a line item), and dumpster and recycling charges (driven by the report sliders).

The crew lead can edit any line item’s label, quantity, rate, unit, and per-line discount percentage; add custom line items; add company charge items from a built-in catalog (fuel surcharge, Big Sky trip fee, dump fee, overtime, and so on); add a Tips line item (a flat-amount line for recording cash or card tips); apply a global discount percentage to the whole bill; and add bill notes.

Submission is gated: the crew lead must check the “Reviewed” checkbox to unlock the report’s Submit button, and the bill cannot be skipped. If the report will not submit, the fix is to open the Bill section and confirm the reviewed box is checked.

Employee Hours card: inside the Job Report, the crew lead logs hours per person for everyone on the job by typing a name, picking the start and end timeline events for that person, and adding any clocked-out periods (lunch, errands, anything subtracted from hours worked) before saving. Hours default to the first START and last FINISH but can be overridden per person. Each saved entry shows worked hours rounded to the nearest quarter hour (5-plus minutes rounds up) alongside the exact actual hours. An entry can be marked “Non-billable” to stay visible on the report without adding to total man-hours. Entries can be edited or removed, and at least two timeline events must be logged before any entry can be added.

The full Job Report fields are: count of personal vehicles on site; dumpster percentage used (0 to 100 slider); recycling percentage used (0 to 100 slider); billing method (invoice, cash, and so on); review candidate (whether to seek a client review: Yes, No, or N/A); hours match (confirming the timeline reflects actual hours billed and paid, with a text field to explain if not); per-person Employee Hours; and optional Crew Feedback to the office.

#### Photos

Photos document jobs and are stored per job in Google Drive. The user opens the Photos tab, taps the camera icon or uploads from the gallery, and adds an optional caption (recommended for each photo). Each photo is automatically compressed to roughly 600KB and queued for upload. Offline photos queue and upload automatically on reconnect; a “stuck” upload indicator means the user is offline and should not repeatedly tap retry. Each job’s photos go to a dedicated Drive folder named Crew Photos/{job_name}_{job_date}/, visible to all crew and admin on that job once synced.

#### Document Library

The Documents tab lets crew browse and download company reference documents from their phone: COIs, blank contracts, bills of lading, estimate templates, policy documents, and more. Crew can browse and download; admin can upload and delete. Downloading requires signal, but browsing the cached list works offline.

#### Expenses and Reimbursement

The Expenses screen lets crew log mileage, request reimbursement for out-of-pocket purchases, and record company-card purchases, replacing the old practice of texting photos of receipts and odometers to Jacob. It opens from the Profile tab and has two modes chosen by a top toggle: Mileage (odometer-based) and Business expense (a personal-card or company-card purchase).

Mileage reimbursement is for driving one’s own vehicle on company business. Fields are date of trip (defaults to today), start odometer, end odometer, start and end odometer photos (new photo or from library), and optional notes. All fields are optional; a partial request (for example, just the two odometer photos) can be submitted, and admin will follow up. The app only blocks a completely empty form and an end odometer reading lower than the start reading.

Business expense (personal or company card) covers any company purchase. Fields are date of expense (defaults to today), amount, category (Fuel, Supplies / Materials, Tolls and Parking, Meals, Equipment Rental, Vehicle Maintenance, Other), vendor (entered as it appears on the receipt), “Paid with” (Personal card requests reimbursement; Company card is log-only with no reimbursement owed), receipt photo, and optional notes. The button reads “Submit request” for a personal-card expense and “Log expense” for a company-card expense.

Every submission appears in a “Your submissions” history list with a status: Pending sync (saved on the phone, not yet uploaded because offline), Submitted (received, awaiting admin review), Approved, or Rejected (with an “Admin:” note to read before talking to Jacob). Behind the scenes, photos upload to Google Drive and each submission is written to a Reimbursements worksheet in the shared Google Sheet, with the row including a link to the photo in Drive. The screen works fully offline; submissions queue and sync automatically on reconnect, photos upload on reconnect, and a submission sitting at “Pending sync” with no signal is normal and goes through on its own.

#### Scheduling Availability

Scheduling Availability is how crew tell the office which days they can work, two weeks at a time, replacing the old practice of texting Jacob. It lives under Profile, then Tools and Resources, then Scheduling Availability.

Availability is submitted in rolling two-week windows, and each day gets one of three statuses: Available (can be scheduled), Unavailable (cannot be scheduled), or Conditional (can be scheduled with a restriction explained in the day’s note, for example “available after 1pm”). Tapping a day cycles it through available, unavailable, conditional, and back to available, and every day in the window must be set before submitting. Once submitted, the crew member is committed: if scheduled on a day marked available, they are expected to work it unless scheduled with 3 or fewer days’ notice, so selections should be double-checked before tapping Submit.

When availability is due, the Submit tab opens directly to the next 14-day window needing input. Quick Fill provides seven day-of-week buttons (Sun through Sat) above the calendar that bulk-set both matching days at once (for example, tapping “Sat” marks both Saturdays unavailable), the fastest way to fill a window with a weekly pattern. Below that, all 14 days display individually, so any single day can be overridden independently of the quick-fill pattern (useful for a one-off Tuesday appointment). Each day can carry an optional note via “+ note” in the Notes card. Once all 14 days are set, the Submit button activates and a confirmation step reminds the user that days inside the next two weeks lock once submitted. Afterward, the next window opens automatically once the submitted horizon drops below two weeks out, so there is no need to remember to come back.

Locked days and unlocks: once a window is submitted, any day inside it within the next two weeks locks against further edits and shows a lock icon, protecting the schedule the office is actively building. To change a locked day, the crew member contacts the office, and Jacob can grant a one-time unlock for a specific 14-day window that reopens every day in it for editing; the office typically revokes the unlock once the updated submission is confirmed. When an unlock is active, a banner explains who granted it and why (if a reason was given), and the window reopens for editing and resubmission like a normal window.

Plan a Future Absence: for a vacation, family event, or anything fixed more than two weeks out, “+ Plan a future absence” at the top of the Submit tab lets the user pre-submit a status for a date range without waiting for the rolling window to reach it. The user picks From and To dates (the start must be at least 14 days out; sooner dates go through the regular submission), picks the status that applies to every day in the range, adds an optional note (for example, “Hawaii vacation”), and submits. The range is recorded immediately, the office sees it today, and it appears in the History tab. Ranges are capped at 100 days per submission; longer stretches are split.

Scheduling notes: a collapsed “Scheduling notes” card below the future-absence button holds one persistent freeform note per person for ongoing constraints that do not fit a single day (for example, “no Saturdays until July” or “mornings only until the 15th”). It autosaves a couple of seconds after typing stops and is always visible to admin alongside availability as a standing heads-up, separate from any single day’s status or note.

History tab: shows every previously submitted window as its own 14-day grid matching what was seen at submit time, including notes, so the crew member can confirm what they sent without remembering it.

Reminder banner: if the submitted availability horizon drops below two weeks out, a banner drops from the top of every screen (except the Scheduling Availability page itself) prompting submission. Tapping Open jumps to the Submit tab; the X dismisses it for the rest of the session, and it reappears next session if the user is still behind.

Offline behavior: filling in days, quick-fill, and notes all work offline, with the in-progress draft saved to the phone. Submitting requires a connection, and a draft survives a lost signal mid-edit, so the user just taps Submit once back online.

#### Estimator (Admin Only)

The Estimator is an in-app moving estimate builder available to admin only. The admin creates an estimate and enters customer info (name, origin, destination), adds rooms, and for each room searches the furniture catalog to add items that auto-fill weight and cubic footage, adjusting quantities as needed and optionally adding photos per estimate; totals in pounds and cubic feet calculate live. The estimator autosaves every field change after a short delay, with a status indicator showing Unsaved, Saving, Saved, or Save failed, so there is no Save button. Catalog items use typeahead search and auto-fill weight and volume; custom items are typed in with weight and volume entered manually; and admin can add, edit, or delete catalog items. Offline, item adds queue and sync when the network returns, but customer details and metadata autosave require connectivity.

#### Admin Features

Admin users have additional tabs and capabilities not visible to crew.

Employees tab: activate newly signed-up accounts, toggle admin role, and deactivate departed crew (admins cannot modify their own record). It also supports Tags (free-form labels such as Driver, Has CC, Tier I, with the available list managed in Settings), Unlocks (one-time exceptions to edit a locked availability window), Emails (alternate addresses, so a calendar invite to a personal address still maps back to the right person in Crew Resources), the Month Schedule View, and a Desktop mode toggle in the admin topbar that widens every admin screen for laptop or desktop viewing, with the preference remembered per device.

Map tab: shows today’s geotagged timeline events on a live map. Crew do not see this tab, and all crew locations logged during the day are operationally visible to admin.

DVIR Review: lists all DVIRs with noted defects requiring mechanic sign-off, with a “Show pending mechanic review only” filter. Defects clear two ways. In person, the admin opens the DVIR, reviews defects, and signs the mechanic portion directly on the device (only admins see the mechanic sign button); once signed, the truck is cleared. Remotely, the admin enters a mechanic’s email (and optionally name) and sends a secure sign-off link scoped to that one DVIR; the mechanic opens it on any device, reviews, and signs with no app account needed, clearing the DVIR exactly as an in-person sign-off would. While a remote request is outstanding, the DVIR shows that a signature was requested and the address it went to, and the request can be re-sent if the email does not arrive.

Office Hours tab: an admin-only panel for logging non-job office or shop time not tied to a customer job. Fields are date, clock in, clock out, any number of break periods entered as start/end pairs (via “+ Add break,” mirroring the Report tab’s employee-hours editor), and optional notes. Net hours calculate automatically (span minus break time); the user does not type the total. Existing entries are listed with Edit and Delete buttons, the panel works offline with entries queuing and showing a “pending sync” count, and office hours land in their own worksheet in the Google Sheet.

Job Summary: the primary admin review surface. Searching by date and customer name pulls a full per-job report including the full timeline of events, DVIRs, materials logged, the job report, the bill with computed total, photos, and admin notes.

Notes: admin can publish two types. Global notes appear as a banner on every crew member’s home screen. Per-job notes appear only when that specific job is selected, used for job-specific instructions (for example, “customer has a piano on the second floor”).

Settings: a Theme and Appearance sub-page holds every look-and-feel control (color preset, font, button shape, button background and size, logo variant, and pin colors), and a current look can be saved as a named custom preset and applied to all users with one tap. Settings also includes help-text overrides for any UI field and pin colors for coloring each event type differently on the map.

Advanced Settings: includes Google Calendar OAuth (upload or replace the token powering the calendar job picker); the DVIR unit list (add, edit, or remove vehicles from the dropdown); Sheet sync refresh (“Refresh sheet from app data,” which recovers events that reached the database but missed the sheet due to a Google API blip, safe to run any time); and an App Health check (“Run health check,” a one-tap snapshot of device state, queued events, database, Google APIs, sheet drift, and env vars that can be copied into a support message). It also hosts Crew Resources Events, a backend integration that automatically posts a daily “Crew Resources” event to a dedicated Resources calendar summarizing who is available that day (grouped by tag, calling out crew leads) alongside who is already scheduled on the Jobs calendar; it runs hourly once enabled, with a “Generate / refresh” button to force an immediate refresh for today through a chosen number of days ahead. A collapsed Diagnostics card at the bottom holds read-only checks for integrations wired through environment variables or OAuth (currently the Crew Resources calendar wiring), opened when a feature looks set up but is not behaving.

Admin scheduling tools: from the Employees tab, “Unlocks” next to any crew member opens their unlock manager, where the admin picks the window-start date (the first day of the 14-day block to reopen), optionally adds a reason, and taps “Grant unlock”; existing unlocks are listed with Revoke buttons, revoked once the person resubmits. The Month Schedule View lays out every active crew member across the top and every day of the selected month down the side, color-coded by availability status, with arrows to move between months. Days where a crew member is actually scheduled on a Jobs-calendar event are called out distinctly, overlaying the availability color, so the admin can see at a glance who is both available and booked, who is booked despite marking unavailable, and where the gaps are; hovering or tapping a cell with a note shows it, and blank cells mean no availability was submitted for that day.
